Start and idle control

With the beak pull disabled, no carton chain or sugar delivery started in 20 seconds. With the pull enabled, cord tension peaked at 1.12 N. The ladle hit the first carton after release.

Cartons, seesaw and bowl

The last carton loaded the seesaw. The marble moved 1 cm in 0.36 s and reached the bridge in 1.64 s. The simulated contacts then proceeded from bridge to feeder to bowl. The rope and bowl bridle passed their separate clearance checks.

Holding the Beetle

The 320 g toy rests against a wood-backed face. Two guide channels transfer the side load into a stand secured to the fixed countertop. The sugar carton balances the bowl. The first-second peak fell from 282 N to 45.0 N when the car began at the gate. The mean sideways load while parked was 0.33 N.

Lifting the gate

The empty bowl is 0.932 kg and the complete gate is 0.962 kg. The 61 g marble gives the bowl a 0.304 N weight advantage. Both runs lifted the gate with 0.060 N of modeled guide drag. A real build would need the assembled carrier weighed and the guide drag measured.

Car impact and lower ramp

The car reached the fixed timber bumper at about 1.08 m/s. Its translational energy was 0.19 J. A separate replay applied the measured bumper impulse to the 9.03 kg ramp along with the wheel loads. Maximum ramp movement during that check was 0.42 mm and 0.02°.

The curved pipes

The original inner and entrance guides stood about 35–51 mm above their intended seats. Their refitted center sections now sit within 0.08 mm of the board at rest. The taller outer bank contains the faster approach. All 59 visible rail mesh placements match the physics stage. The marble stayed on the channel side of the rails in both runs.

Checking contact

The native mechanism ran at 3,840 Hz. I checked interpolated replay geometry at 1,920 samples per second around the bend. The worst filmed-run overlap of the marble’s bounding sphere with a rendered pipe surface was 0.30 mm. The sphere did not emerge through a pipe. Finite-step rigid-body solvers permit slight penetration during contact.

Boards and surrounding solids

All 19 elevated boards passed the settling and load checks. The largest board displacement from its settled pose over the whole filmed run was 0.22 mm. The wider scene check sampled 485 groups and found no unexplained intersections. The cord’s smallest measured unintended-obstacle clearance was 0.72 mm.

Coffee and celebration

The sugar stayed in the mug in both the 1,920 Hz and 1,280 Hz support runs. Cup contact occurred at 13.35 s and 13.53 s, respectively. In the filmed run, Microduck watched the sugar, then turned using its torque-limited head joints, nodded twice and rocked its body. Its lens held within 2.14° of the camera for the measured 1.15 s interval.

First marble at rest

At 960 Hz the first marble remained at rest before impact but stalled on the bowl feeder after release. The earlier run drifted 37.0 mm before the carton hit. This run starts the free marble in the resting pose reached under gravity in the idle control. With the pull disabled, its maximum displacement over 20 seconds was 0.000 mm. In the film it moved at most 0.000 mm before impact and then travelled 10 mm in 0.36 seconds. Motion measurements

Physical limits

These are simulation results. The gate guide and bolted joints are ideal constraints. The largest short modeled contacts were 144 N at the lifting gate and 736 N at the bumper. The model does not establish the pull-out strength of the fasteners, impact strength of the wood, or crushing strength of individual pantry tins. The can-load screen assumes intact steel of the modeled thickness. The coffee surface marks liquid entry and the mug catches the cube. Fluid flow and dissolution are outside the model.
